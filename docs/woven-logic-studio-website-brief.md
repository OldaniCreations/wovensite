# Woven Logic Studio Website Brief

**Status:** Working brief for v1 build  
**Primary site:** Woven Logic Studio  
**Principal:** Tristin  
**Primary goal:** Help the right people recognize a difficult problem, understand how Tristin can help, and start a conversation before they have a perfectly defined brief.

## 1. Brand premise

**Complex problems don't fit neatly into boxes. Neither do the best ways of solving them.**

Woven Logic Studio is an independent problem-solving practice for teams working on things that do not have obvious answers. Woven follows the problem rather than beginning with a fixed discipline or deliverable. The work may combine research, strategy, design, technology, experimentation, and storytelling—whatever the problem needs to understand what is really happening, make possibilities tangible, test what works, and turn it into something useful.

Woven is not an umbrella for unrelated projects and it is not a generic innovation consultancy. It is a recognizable way of working: get inside the problem, question assumptions, connect what others may not, make something real enough to learn from, and change direction when the evidence says to.

## 2. Tristin and Woven

The site should make Tristin visible immediately. Woven is the practice she built to work in this way; it is not an anonymous agency layer between the visitor and the person doing the work.

Working relationship to communicate:

- **Tristin** is the person, point of view, and primary collaborator.
- **Woven Logic Studio** is the container, method, and evolving body of work.
- **Partners and specialists** can be brought in when the problem calls for them, but the core promise is Tristin's cross-disciplinary thinking and making.

Homepage explanation:

> I'm Tristin. I work across research, strategy, design, technology, experimentation and storytelling, using whatever the problem needs to uncover what's really happening, create and test solutions, and turn what works into something useful.

> **Woven Logic Studio is the practice I built to work that way.**

The About page can hold the fuller Intel and professional history, education, community work, speaking, and awards. The homepage only needs to answer: who is Tristin, why does she work this way, and why does Woven exist?

## 3. Target visitor and problem-recognition model

The primary visitor is a founder, product/design leader, innovation or program leader, or executive responsible for a meaningful problem where the answer is not obvious. Startups are a natural fit because they often offer high agency, talented collaborators, momentum, and room to experiment; larger organizations with well-sponsored, interesting problems also fit.

Do not ask visitors to diagnose which professional service they need. They may not know they need service design, research, AI, prototyping, or strategy. They know something is unclear, stuck, failing, or important.

The site should use familiar terms for discovery, Woven language for recognition, and the work as proof.

| Visitor situation                                     | Woven engagement | Immediate promise                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "We don't really know what's going on."               | **Explore**      | Find clarity about the real problem or opportunity.                            |
| "We have an idea, but we don't know if it will work." | **Prove**        | Create evidence before committing heavily; the answer may be "don't build it." |
| "What we have isn't working anymore."                 | **Transform**    | Rethink the product, service, process, or system beyond incremental fixes.     |
| "We need someone inside this with us."                | **Embed**        | Add cross-disciplinary thinking and making capacity to move the work forward.  |

## 6. Explore / Prove / Transform / Embed

These are ways Woven can engage with a problem, not rigid packages or project categories. Each deeper page or section should describe the situation, likely activities, useful outputs, and what a client can learn or decide—not a fake fixed process.

### Explore — clarity

For problems or opportunities that are poorly understood. Work can include interviews, observation, systems mapping, journey and ecosystem work, data review, synthesis, and reframing. Outputs might include a clearer problem definition, opportunity areas, shared understanding, and a focused direction.

### Prove — evidence

For promising ideas that need to become tangible before major investment. Work can include prototyping, experiments, technical exploration, user testing, service walkthroughs, and measurement. The result may be a validated direction, a sharper next step, or a confident decision not to build.

### Transform — change

For existing products, services, processes, or systems that have accumulated friction and workarounds. Work can combine research, strategy, experience design, technology, and organizational alignment to find where the real problems live and create a better way of working.

### Embed — capability

For teams that need a cross-disciplinary thinking-and-making partner inside the work. Tristin can join an existing initiative, connect teams and disciplines, challenge assumptions, make ideas tangible, and help the team move with more clarity and momentum.

## 7. Featured proof projects

The homepage should feature six projects, with the first three given the greatest visual weight. The purpose is not to show everything Tristin has done; it is to let visitors recognize the pattern underneath the varied work.

### Home Energy Manager

**Question:** How can a home make its own energy use understandable enough to act on?

A personal experiment connecting solar, batteries, household energy data, automation, and AI into one system designed to help people understand what their home is doing and make better decisions about energy use.

**Proves:** Tristin can enter a messy real-world system, teach herself what is needed, integrate technologies, experiment, and build something useful.

### Please, Health Me!?

**Question:** How do you make healthcare easier to understand without oversimplifying it?

A healthcare education podcast and resource platform built around real questions people have when medicine, testing, insurance, and everyday life get confusing. It combines clinical expertise with research, storytelling, digital tools, and practical resources.

**Proves:** Research, storytelling, technology, and experience design can make complex expertise more approachable and useful.

### AI Developer Assistant

**Question:** What if the documentation isn't actually the problem?

Thousands of engineers were losing time to setup friction and scattered knowledge. Tristin reframed the issue as knowledge access, then designed a RAG-powered assistant grounded in real support data, alongside onboarding and an AI quality framework. Developer experience scores rose from 59% to 75%, and setup time was cut in half.

**Proves:** Tristin can reframe the stated problem, use emerging technology appropriately, and connect research, AI, strategy, and experience design to measurable improvement.

### Carbon Aware AI Workload Manager

**Question:** How do you turn sustainability research into something developers can actually use?

Working with Intel Labs, Tristin translated carbon-aware computing research into a working system that helped AI developers see emissions and schedule workloads around renewable-energy availability, reducing carbon impact by as much as 60%.

**Proves:** She can connect research, sustainability, infrastructure, systems, design, and technology into a usable solution.

### Smart Edge Developer Experience

**Question:** How do you create clarity around a technology almost nobody understands yet?

As Intel's edge platform entered a major transformation, Tristin worked across 16+ roles, developer journeys, documentation, business goals, and product strategy to understand the emerging ecosystem and create a clearer path for developers entering it.

**Proves:** She can enter an unfamiliar domain, synthesize a complex ecosystem, and create clarity and direction across teams.

### Stat Doctors

**Question:** What if the real solution is to remove the system everyone assumes you need?

A virtual healthcare startup relied on a costly call center that frequently failed to connect patients and doctors. Tristin helped replace it with a custom Twilio-based paging system and redesigned the supporting physician and administrative experiences. Missed connections and disruptions dropped by 95%, while the call-center cost was eliminated.

**Proves:** This way of working is not new: question the assumed solution, connect technology and experience design, build a different system, and measure the result.

## 8. Voice and tone

Sound like an intelligent, curious, practical person who is comfortable in complexity and cares whether the outcome helps people.

- Direct, specific, and human.
- Warm without being folksy; confident without being grandiose.
- Curious and challenging, especially around assumptions.
- Concrete about what gets done and what changes.
- Comfortable with technical language when it clarifies; never use jargon as a substitute for meaning.
- Show usefulness, evidence, and learning—not innovation theater.

Avoid generic consultancy language such as "human-centered innovation at the intersection of strategy, design, and technology," inflated claims, long service menus, and copy that makes Woven sound like theory without making.

## 10. Visual and brand questions to validate

Treat the existing logo, palette, type, graphic elements, and other brand materials as placeholders to evaluate—not as assets that must automatically be replaced.

Validate whether the current system:

- Feels connected, layered, and intelligent without becoming literal "woven" decoration.
- Has enough warmth and personality for a human-led practice while retaining rigor and credibility.
- Can support a large, provocative hero headline and dense case-study information.
- Makes the six capabilities and proof questions easy to scan.
- Works at small sizes, in navigation, social previews, contact forms, and case-study layouts.
- Gives professional work and personal experiments a coherent visual home without flattening their differences.
- Supports restrained motion or interaction that reveals relationships and evidence rather than adding decoration.
- Remains accessible: strong contrast, readable type, clear focus states, reduced-motion support, and meaningful image alternatives.

The first brand exercise should be an evidence-based fit check: place the current identity against the hero, one problem-recognition section, and one proof card. Keep, adapt, or replace elements only after seeing whether they help the story land.

## 11. CTA strategy

Use one primary action throughout the site:

**Tell me what you're trying to solve →**

Supporting CTA language can vary by context—"Bring me a problem," "Start with the problem," or "Have a problem you're trying to solve?"—but should always reduce the pressure to arrive with a formal brief. The contact flow should ask for the situation, what is known, what is stuck, timing, and how to follow up; it should not force visitors to choose a service category first.

The CTA should appear in the hero, after the engagement model, after proof, and in the footer. Keep it visible in the navigation on desktop and mobile without competing with the story.

## 12. Implementation notes

- **Initial routes:** Home, Work, individual case study template, About, and Contact. Add dedicated Explore / Prove / Transform / Embed pages when the offer detail is ready; the homepage can introduce them first.
- **Content model:** Store projects as structured entries with title, problem/question, summary, proof statement, engagement mode, capability tags, context, outcomes, images, and links. This keeps the homepage and deeper case studies in sync.
- **Component system:** Build reusable Hero, ProblemCard, EngagementCard, CapabilityList, ProofCard, CaseStudyHeader, OutcomeMetric, AboutIntro, and CTA components.
- **Responsive behavior:** The headline, problem cards, project questions, and CTA must remain clear at narrow widths. Do not rely on hover to reveal essential content.
- **Case-study priority:** Lead with the problem, then context, approach, what was made, evidence/outcome, and what the project proves about the Woven method.
- **SEO and sharing:** Use plain-language page titles and descriptions that include recognizable terms such as research, strategy, experience design, prototyping, AI, developer experience, systems, and healthcare. Generate useful social previews from each case study.
- **Accessibility:** Use semantic headings, keyboard-accessible links and cards, alt text, sufficient contrast, visible focus, form labels, and reduced-motion behavior.
- **Measurement:** Track primary CTA clicks, contact starts/completions, engagement-mode clicks, case-study views, and outbound project links. Use these signals to learn what visitors recognize and what needs clarification.
- **Build sequence:** Lock content structure and hero; test the existing visual identity on representative sections; build the homepage shell; create the case-study template; add About and Contact; then expand the engagement pages.

## Definition of done for v1

A visitor should be able to understand within 30 seconds that Tristin works on difficult, cross-disciplinary problems; recognize which situation resembles theirs; see credible evidence; and know exactly how to begin without first knowing what service to request.
